    \subsubsection{Q1 -- Results}\label{q1-results}

\textbf{Method.} The image was loaded with IMREAD\_REDUCED\_COLOR\_4
(360×360) and converted to grayscale. Scale-normalised LoG responses
σ²·∇²(G\_σ * I) were computed for \textbf{σ = 2.0 to 16.27} (16 scales,
geometric ratio 1.15). Sunflower centres are dark blobs, so the signed
response is kept (positive peaks). A 3×3×3 local maximum in (scale, y,
x) with response ≥ 60 marks a blob, with radius r = √2·σ. Detections
above the field (y ≤ 180) were discarded and overlapping centres
suppressed. \texttt{cv2.HoughCircles} was not used.

\textbf{Result} A total of 63 candidate blobs were detected, and 52
circles remained after filtering.

The RANSAC line (magenta) and RANSAC circle (magenta) lie very close to

    \subsubsection{(d) What if the circle is fitted
first?}\label{d-what-if-the-circle-is-fitted-first}

When the circle is fitted first using all 100 points, RANSAC finds
\textbf{60 inliers: 50 circle points and 10 line points}. The fitted
circle parameters, (1.979, 2.966, 9.958), are still close to the true
values. Therefore, the circle fit is not affected much.

However, 10 line points near the circle crossings are also removed. As a
result, only 40 of the original 50 line points remain for fitting the
line.

Since both the circle and the line have 50 points, fitting the circle
first works reasonably well in this case. However, points from the other
shape can affect the fitting result. Fitting the line first is safer
because fewer circle points fall within the line's inlier band.

    \subsubsection{(e) Why do the USB-B port, capacitors and reset button
differ
strongly?}\label{e-why-do-the-usb-b-port-capacitors-and-reset-button-differ-strongly}

A homography works well only for points on the same flat plane, which is
the PCB surface in this case. The USB-B port, capacitors and reset
button are raised above the board. When the camera moves, these parts
shift relative to the PCB due to parallax, so the same homography cannot
align them correctly.

Their shiny surfaces also reflect light differently depending on the
camera angle and lighting. Shadows, lens distortion and keypoint
detection errors also contribute to the differences. Flat features, such
as printed text and PCB traces, align well, so they appear darker in the
difference image.
